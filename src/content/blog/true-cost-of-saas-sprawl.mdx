## The $135,000 Problem

The average mid-size company (100-500 employees) uses 130 SaaS tools and spends $4,000 per employee annually on software. For a 200-person company, that is $800,000 per year.

But here is the number that should keep you up at night: 30% of that spend is waste. Unused licenses, redundant tools, and enterprise features nobody needs. That is $240,000 per year going to software vendors for nothing.

SaaS sprawl is not a technology problem. It is a procurement problem. Teams buy tools independently, nobody tracks what overlaps, and auto-renewals happen without review. The result is a stack of 130 tools where 40 of them do the same thing.

This guide shows you how to audit your SaaS stack in 2 hours, identify the waste, and eliminate it without losing critical functionality.

## Step 1: Build Your SaaS Inventory

You cannot optimize what you do not measure. Start by building a complete inventory of every SaaS tool your company pays for.

**Where to find the data:**

**Expense reports:** Pull all software-related expenses from your accounting system. Look for recurring charges from SaaS vendors.

**Corporate credit card statements:** Search for recurring monthly and annual charges. Many small tools are purchased on credit cards without going through procurement.

**SSO/Identity provider:** If you use Okta, Auth0, or Google Workspace, you can see which apps employees have authenticated with. This is the most complete list of tools in use.

**Ask department heads:** Send a simple form to every department: "List every SaaS tool your team pays for, including the number of seats and monthly cost."

**What to capture for each tool:**

| Field | Why It Matters |
|-------|---------------|
| Tool name | Identification |
| Category | Grouping and redundancy detection |
| Number of licenses | Utilization tracking |
| Monthly/annual cost | Financial impact |
| Contract end date | Renewal timing |
| Department owner | Accountability |
| Primary use case | Redundancy detection |

## Step 2: Measure Actual Utilization

Once you have your inventory, measure how each tool is actually used.

**License utilization:** Most SaaS tools have an admin dashboard showing active users vs. allocated seats. Pull this number for every tool. The industry benchmark is 70% utilization — anything below that is waste.

**Feature utilization:** Which features does your team actually use? Many teams pay for Business or Enterprise plans but only use features available in the Starter tier.

**Login frequency:** How often do employees log in? A tool with 50 licenses but only 20 weekly active users has significant waste.

**Data volume:** For tools that charge by usage (storage, API calls, emails sent), pull your actual usage numbers and compare to your plan limits.

## Step 3: Identify Redundancies

This is where the real savings happen. Most companies have 3-5 categories where they use multiple tools that do the same thing.

**Common redundancy patterns:**

**Project management:** Trello + Asana + Monday + Notion. Each team picks their own tool, but they all manage tasks and projects. Consolidate to one.

**Communication:** Slack + Microsoft Teams + Discord. Real-time messaging is real-time messaging. Pick one.

**CRM:** HubSpot + Salesforce + Pipedrive. Multiple sales teams or regions using different CRMs. Consolidate to one.

**Design:** Figma + Sketch + Adobe XD. Designers pick their preferred tool, but they all create UI designs. Standardize on one.

**Monitoring:** Datadog + New Relic + Grafana. Different teams monitoring different things with overlapping tools. Consolidate.

**How to detect redundancies:**

For each tool in your inventory, ask: "Is there another tool in our stack that does the same thing?" Use our Stack Auditor to automate this — it cross-references feature matrices to identify exact overlaps.

## Step 5: Prioritize Quick Wins

Not all waste is equal. Prioritize by impact and effort:

**Quick wins (1-2 hours):**
- Cancel unused licenses
- Downgrade over-provisioned plans
- Remove duplicate tools in the same category

**Medium effort (1-2 weeks):**
- Negotiate renewal contracts with usage data
- Consolidate redundant tools
- Migrate to cheaper alternatives

**Long-term projects (1-3 months):**
- Replace enterprise tools with open source alternatives
- Implement centralized procurement
- Build a SaaS management process

## Step 6: Build a Governance Process

Auditing once is good. Auditing continuously is better. Build a process to prevent future sprawl:

**Centralized procurement:** All SaaS purchases go through one person or team. No more shadow IT.

**Quarterly reviews:** Review your SaaS stack every quarter. Check utilization, identify new redundancies, and renegotiate contracts.

**Renewal calendar:** Track every contract end date. Start renewal negotiations 90 days before expiration.

**Usage dashboards:** Build dashboards that show SaaS utilization across the company. Make waste visible to leadership.
