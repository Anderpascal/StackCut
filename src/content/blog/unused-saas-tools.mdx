## The Silent Budget Killer

After auditing hundreds of SaaS stacks, one pattern emerges with depressing consistency: most organizations pay for at least 30 percent more software than they actually need. The waste falls into three categories: redundant tools with overlapping capabilities, unused paid seats, and over-provisioned plan tiers.

Here are the five most common offenders we encounter, and what to do about each one.

## 1. Project Management Overlap (Trello + ClickUp + Notion + Asana + Monday)

It is shockingly common to find teams using three or even four project management tools simultaneously. Marketing uses Asana, engineering uses Jira, product uses Linear, and someone in operations is still maintaining a Trello board from 2019.

The cost per tool seems small individually — $10 to $25 per user per month — but a 50-person company paying for four project management tools at $15 per seat burns $3,000 per month on redundant capability.

### The Fix

Consolidate on one or two tools maximum. Use the Stack Auditor on StackCut to identify exact feature overlap between your tools. Chances are you can drop to one project management tool and one lightweight task tracker.

## 2. Communication Tool Overload (Slack + Teams + Discord + Google Chat)

This one sneaks up because communication tools feel essential and their per-user pricing is modest. But Slack at $7.25 per user, Microsoft Teams bundled with M365, Discord at $10 for Nitro, and Google Chat included with Workspace mean you are paying for four tools that do the same thing.

The real cost is not just the subscription fees — it is the fragmentation. Messages get lost across platforms, context switching kills productivity, and search becomes useless when your conversation history is scattered across four apps.

### The Fix

Pick one primary communication platform and shut down the others. If your organization is on Microsoft 365, use Teams. If you are on Google Workspace, use Google Chat. If you need Slack's integrations, commit to Slack. Do not try to run all of them simultaneously.

## 3. CRM Redundancy (HubSpot + Salesforce + Pipedrive)

Yes, we have seen companies running HubSpot for marketing, Salesforce for sales, and Pipedrive for a specific team that did not want to use either. CRM overlap is expensive because these tools charge premium per-user rates — $25 to $165 per user per month.

A 40-person sales team on Salesforce Enterprise at $165 per user pays $79,200 annually. Add 20 marketing users on HubSpot Professional at $800 per month, and you are looking at over $88,000 per year. If a subset of the team is also using Pipedrive Advanced at $29 per user, that is another $13,920 per year for 20 users.

### The Fix

Choose one CRM platform. Migrate data, train everyone on the same tool, and cancel the rest. The migration is painful, but the savings compound year after year.

## 4. Support Tool Sprawl (Zendesk + Intercom + Crisp + Freshdesk)

Support tools have become a battleground of overlapping features. Intercom started as a chat widget but now does ticketing and knowledge base. Zendesk has chat, ticketing, and CRM features. Freshdesk covers the same ground at a lower price point.

A mid-size company might run Zendesk for ticketing ($55 per agent), Intercom for chat ($74 per seat), and Freshdesk for a specific product line ($15 per agent). That is $144 per support person per month before you count knowledge base tools and chatbots.

### The Fix

Audit your support tool feature usage. Most teams use less than 40 percent of what they pay for. Consolidate on the one tool that covers 80 percent of your actual needs and supplement the gaps with lightweight alternatives.

## 5. Email Marketing Redundancy (Mailchimp + ConvertKit + ActiveCampaign)

Email marketing tools are notorious for feature creep and pricing jumps based on subscriber count. Companies often end up paying for multiple platforms because teams segment their audiences: marketing uses Mailchimp for newsletters, sales uses ActiveCampaign for sequences, and the content team uses ConvertKit for creator workflows.

A company with 10,000 contacts on Mailchimp Standard at $100 per month, 5,000 on ActiveCampaign Plus at $89 per month, and 3,000 on ConvertKit Creator Pro at $59 per month spends $2,976 per year on redundant email infrastructure.

### The Fix

Consolidate email lists into a single platform. The migration cost is a one-time effort. The annual savings are permanent. Most modern email platforms can handle newsletters, automations, and sequences within a single account.
