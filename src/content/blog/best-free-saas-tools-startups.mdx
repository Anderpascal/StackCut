## Spend Zero Until the Problem Is Real

Every dollar you spend on software before product-market fit is a dollar that did not go into engineering, sales, or runway. The good news: in 2026, the free tier of most major SaaS categories is genuinely usable for teams under 10–15 people. The bad news: free tiers are designed to create upgrade pressure, and most startups end up paying for things they could have avoided.

This guide covers the strongest free and freemium options by category, what the real limits are, and when it actually makes sense to upgrade — or switch to a cheaper paid alternative. If your current stack is already costing you money, compare what you are paying against [StackCut's alternatives finder](/alternatives) before reading further.

## CRM: Managing Relationships Before You Have a CRM Budget

A full-featured CRM is not required at the 0-to-10 customer stage. A spreadsheet, a shared inbox, and good habits will carry you further than a misconfigured Salesforce instance.

That said, when you do need structure, the freemium CRM market is strong. HubSpot's free CRM tier offers unlimited contacts, a deal pipeline, and basic email tracking — enough for most seed-stage sales motions. Zoho CRM's free plan covers up to 3 users with leads, contacts, and deal stages. Notion and Airtable both serve as lightweight CRM replacements with free plans that handle pipeline views, contact records, and basic automation.

The upgrade trigger: when you need sequences, automated follow-ups, or multi-rep assignment rules, free tiers start to constrain you. At that point, do a proper cost comparison — do not default to HubSpot's first paid tier just because it is the next button in their upgrade flow.

## Project Management: Ship Without Paying for Seats

Linear, Notion, Trello, and Asana all offer free tiers. For small teams, Linear's free plan is particularly strong — unlimited issues, cycles, and roadmap views for up to 250 issues. Notion's free plan covers unlimited pages and blocks for individuals, with collaboration available for small teams at a low cost.

Trello's free tier gives you unlimited cards and up to 10 boards, which covers most teams through early product stages. Asana's free tier covers unlimited tasks and projects for up to 15 users, with basic list and board views.

The tool that often goes overlooked: GitHub Projects. If your team already lives in GitHub, the built-in project boards cover backlog management, sprint tracking, and milestone views — at zero additional cost. Do not pay for a separate project management tool if your issues are already in GitHub.

## Communication: Async First, Expensive Second

Slack's free tier retains only the last 90 days of message history, which is a real constraint for teams that use it for documentation. Discord is increasingly used by technical startups as a Slack alternative — unlimited history, free forever, and strong for async threads.

For async video, Loom's free tier allows up to 25 videos with 5-minute recording limits, which covers most async update and bug reproduction use cases. Linear's updates, Notion, and GitHub Discussions can handle much of what teams reach for Slack to do — at no cost.

The honest upgrade case for Slack: when history loss is creating genuine coordination failures or compliance requirements. Below that threshold, the free tier is sufficient for most startups through Series A.

## Email Marketing: Start Free, Switch Smart

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) offers a free plan with unlimited contacts and up to 300 emails per day — roughly 9,000 per month. Mailchimp's free tier covers 500 contacts and 1,000 sends per month. Both are viable for early list-building and pre-launch campaigns.

Resend and Loops are developer-focused email platforms with generous free tiers that integrate cleanly with modern stacks — worth evaluating if your team is engineering-led.

Be cautious about growing deeply into a free email marketing tier and then being surprised by the upgrade cost at scale. Some platforms have steep pricing cliffs between free and first paid tier. When you start approaching the contact limit, shop the market rather than defaulting to the incumbent's paid plan. [StackCut's alternatives tool](/alternatives) covers email marketing comparisons.

## Dev Tools: Most of the Stack Can Be Free

The core developer toolchain — version control, CI/CD, code review, secrets management — has strong free options.

GitHub Free covers unlimited public and private repos, GitHub Actions at 2,000 minutes/month, and GitHub Pages. Vercel and Netlify both have free tiers that cover most early-stage frontend deployments. PlanetScale, Supabase, Neon, and Railway all have free database tiers with meaningful limits for development and early production.

For monitoring and observability, Sentry's free tier covers 5,000 errors/month with 30-day retention. BetterStack has a free log management tier. Axiom offers generous free log ingestion for small teams.

The one area where free tiers are thin: production-grade infrastructure at any meaningful traffic volume. Cloud provider free tiers (AWS, GCP, Azure) have explicit 12-month or usage limits. Do not build your startup's architecture around free-tier assumptions you will breach in month 6.

## Design: Figma's Free Tier Is Actually Generous

Figma's Starter plan allows unlimited personal drafts and up to 3 collaborative design files. For a solo designer or a founding team doing their own design work, this covers most early-stage UI and marketing work.

Canva's free tier remains one of the strongest in design tooling — templates, brand kits (limited), and basic exports cover the needs of most non-design teams producing marketing materials, decks, and social content.

When you have a dedicated designer and need version history, branching, and unlimited projects, the upgrade case is clear. Until then, free tiers hold up.

## Analytics: Measure Before You Pay to Measure

Google Analytics 4 is free and covers most early-stage web analytics needs. PostHog has a generous free self-hosted option and a cloud plan with 1 million events/month free — strong for product analytics, funnels, and session recording without a Mixpanel or Amplitude bill.

Umami and Plausible are lightweight, privacy-first analytics tools with self-hosted free options, worth considering if GDPR compliance is a concern.

The upgrade trigger: when you need advanced retention analysis, multi-touch attribution, or data warehouse connectors, free tiers across all analytics platforms will constrain you. At that point, model the cost of the paid tier against the decisions it will actually enable.

## When Free Tiers Become Expensive

The irony of over-relying on free tiers: the switching cost accumulates. Data migrations, re-integrations, and team retraining when you eventually have to upgrade or switch can cost more in engineering time than the SaaS bill would have. The right approach is to use free tiers aggressively until you hit a genuine business constraint — then shop deliberately rather than defaulting to the same vendor's paid plan.

If you are already paying for tools that have strong free alternatives, run a [StackCut downgrade analysis](/downgrade) to see which tools are candidates for a free-tier switch or a cheaper alternative. Many startups are six months into a paid plan for something that a free tier would handle adequately at their current scale.

## Bottom Line

The best free SaaS tools are not consolation prizes — they are the right tools for the right stage. Use them aggressively, know their real limits, and upgrade only when the constraint is costing you more than the subscription. The worst outcome is paying enterprise prices for startup problems.
