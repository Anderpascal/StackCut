## Why Pay for What Is Free?

Enterprise SaaS vendors have perfected the art of bundling. They take three or four security features that most large organizations need — SAML Single Sign-On, audit logging, role-based access control, and uptime monitoring — and lock them behind the most expensive plan tier.

The result is the "enterprise tax": a 200 to 400 percent price increase per user, justified by features that should cost a fraction of that premium. This post walks through the open source alternatives that eliminate the enterprise tax entirely.

## SAML Single Sign-On: Keycloak vs. Authentik vs. Dex

SSO is the number one reason companies upgrade to enterprise plans. Every major SaaS vendor gates SAML authentication behind their enterprise tier. The good news: open source identity providers have matured to the point where they are viable even for regulated industries.

### Keycloak

Keycloak is the grandfather of open source identity and access management. Maintained by Red Hat, it supports SAML 2.0, OpenID Connect, OAuth 2.0, LDAP, and Active Directory. It handles user federation, social login, and multi-factor authentication.

Setup takes about 40 hours for a mid-size deployment. The learning curve is real, but once running, Keycloak can front-end every SaaS application in your stack — eliminating the need for enterprise SSO across all your tools.

### Authentik

Authentik is a newer, more polished alternative. Its admin UI is modern and intuitive, making setup significantly faster than Keycloak — about 20 hours for a basic deployment. It supports the same protocols and integrates well with existing infrastructure.

If you are deploying SSO for the first time and do not have a dedicated identity team, Authentik is the better starting point.

### Dex

Dex is the lightweight option. It does not handle user management directly — instead, it acts as an OIDC and SAML proxy, connecting your applications to upstream identity providers like Google, GitHub, or LDAP. Perfect for Kubernetes-native environments and organizations that already have an identity source of truth.

## Audit Logs: Grafana Loki and Vector

Audit logging is the second most common enterprise-gate feature. Vendors justify the premium by pointing to compliance requirements — SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR — that demand comprehensive activity logging.

### Grafana Loki

Loki is a horizontally-scalable, highly-available log aggregation system designed for cost efficiency. Unlike traditional log platforms that index full text, Loki indexes only metadata (labels), dramatically reducing storage costs.

For audit logging, Loki provides searchable, queryable log streams with retention policies and alerting — everything you need for compliance without the per-user pricing.

### Vector

Vector is a lightweight, high-performance observability data pipeline. It can collect, transform, and route logs from any source to any destination. Combined with Loki, it creates a complete audit trail infrastructure that costs a fraction of enterprise audit features.

## Authorization: Casbin and Open Policy Agent

Custom roles and permissions are another common enterprise feature that open source handles well.

### Casbin

Casbin is a powerful authorization library supporting ACL, RBAC, ABAC, and custom models. It works across Go, Java, Node.js, Python, .NET, and more. Companies like Intel, VMware, and IBM use Casbin in production.

### Open Policy Agent (OPA)

OPA is a policy engine that decouples authorization logic from application code. Write policies in Rego (OPA's policy language), deploy them as a sidecar or daemon, and enforce consistent authorization across your entire stack. Used by Netflix, Pinterest, and Goldman Sachs.

## Uptime Monitoring: Uptime Kuma

Many enterprise plans include SLA guarantees backed by uptime monitoring. Uptime Kuma is a self-hosted alternative that provides beautiful status pages, multi-channel alerts (email, Slack, Discord, Telegram), and comprehensive uptime tracking.

Setup takes about eight hours. The ongoing cost is whatever it costs to run a small Docker container — essentially free compared to adding an enterprise tier for SLA guarantees.

## The Economics

Let us run the numbers. A 200-person company on a Professional plan at $40 per user per month spends $96,000 per year. Upgrading to Enterprise at $120 per user for SSO and audit logs costs $288,000 per year — a $192,000 premium.

Deploying Keycloak, Loki, Casbin, and Uptime Kuma requires roughly 100 hours of engineering time. If your DevOps engineers cost $100 per hour, the one-time setup cost is $10,000. Year one savings: $182,000. Every subsequent year: $192,000.

That is not an enterprise tax. That is a choice.
